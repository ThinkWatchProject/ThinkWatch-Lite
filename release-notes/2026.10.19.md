**Upgrade notes:**
- The bundled core is now 0.73.0, with control-plane protocol 48. A remote server must run core 0.73.0; servers on 0.72.x do not connect until they are upgraded.
- Nothing is cleared; the request history, stored payloads and settings carry over, and every existing rule keeps its behaviour.

**Security page:**
- The three protections are now named for what they guard against: **Secrets & privacy** (formerly Outbound redaction), **Dangerous commands** (formerly Tool-call inspection) and **Content filter**. Each has three states, **On**, **Monitor** and **Off**, shown as a dot on its tab.
- **Monitor** records every match in the log and raises a system notification, and changes nothing. **Record only** on a single rule does the same under On. The same rule notifies at most once every 5 minutes, matches arriving together are merged into one notification, and a notification never contains the matched text.
- Every rule has its own switch and action, set directly in the list: Secrets & privacy rules replace with a placeholder or record only; Dangerous commands rules cut off or record only; Content filter rules refuse, delete, replace or record only. While a protection is in Monitor, rules show their action with "Monitoring" next to it.
- Built-in rules are grouped by category, with a checkbox on each group to switch all of its rules at once, and a search box filters rules by name and pattern.
- **Match case** in the Secrets & privacy rule dialog is now a switch.

**Content filter for answers:**
- A content rule now says what it checks: **Request**, **Answer** or **Request and answer**. A rule on answers checks the text the model writes, including Codex's freeform tool input; tool calls stay with Dangerous commands, and thinking is not checked.
- **Replace** writes the rule's replacement text (default `***`) in place of the match, on requests and answers. It is not undone in the answer. **Delete** removes the match without a trace.
- Answers keep streaming: only the few characters that could still become a match are held back. A rule on answers cannot refuse and cannot match code points; those options are disabled in the dialog with the reason given.
- The test box tries a sample as a request or as an answer.
- While a rule on answers can delete or replace, token probabilities (logprobs) are left out of streamed answers.

**Log and traffic:**
- Matches in answers show where they were found, and the outcome says how many places were deleted or replaced. Requests whose text was replaced carry a **Replaced** mark in the traffic list.
- Secrets that a Record only rule let through are counted as such instead of being shown as redacted.

**Fixed:**
- Rule dialogs fit small windows and scroll inside instead of pushing the buttons out of view.
